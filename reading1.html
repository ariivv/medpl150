Reading Responses

Reading 1: "In Plato’s Cave" – Susan Sontag

Susan Sontag’s “In Plato’s Cave” made me think differently about photography in our era. Before reading this article, I mostly thought of photography to capture the moment and keep memories. Sontag argues that photography is more than that. She explains how photographs shape the way people think and understand the world around them and their environment. Although this article was written before social media; her points are valid in today's society. Something that stood out to me was her claim that people don’t often live life in the moment if they are constantly thinking about getting the right photo. This is a person's way of controlling experiences. Taking pictures in a way that tries to make people feel your emotions is an example of this. You can also control how people perceive your life through images posted on social media. For example, capturing certain moments that may seem luxurious or expensive and solely posting this kind of content can create certain thoughts about you in someone's head. I often do find myself losing the plot of living in the moment, by trying to capture memories. If I’m recording something, I’ll often watch it through my phone and not look up as it’s happening in real life. I believe many younger people in this generation have been living through experiences this way unfortunately. Sontag believes that by doing this we’ll detach ourselves from reality. As someone who is interested in photography and videography, I totally agree with Sontag’s claims. And reading this has opened my eyes and has made me aware of how not in-tune i can be with my environment . But with this being said, I don’t want to change the way I capture moments through footage. I’d like to continue trying to evoke certain emotions throughout my work. I understand that art is subjective and interpreted individually. Yet, as an artist I believe that somewhat manipulating your audience's emotions through your work is what makes everyone's art unique. Sontang may have changed the way I live through experiences while simultaneously trying to capture footage, but her claims haven’t changed the way I'd like to continue making art. I don’t take capture moments for proof; I capture moments with emotion.
