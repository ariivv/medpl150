Reading Responses

Reading 2: ‘AI slop’ distorting our reality, the world is sleepwalking into disaster - Nesrine Malik

These stories show how motherhood in Seirra Leone can come with many dangers and uncertainties. In the first story Memunatu: “Birth in My Village” was about giving birth in an isolated village, where healthcare isn’t common. The women of this village often give birth at home with a “lie lie” doctor, more like an herbalist. She had a life-threatening birth where she unfortunately lost her baby and was ill for the following 9 months. Her story highlights the healthcare inequality women, especially pregnant, face in this country. The next story was Heela: “My stolen future” is about a teenage schoolgirl who was raped an 18-year-old who claimed to be her boyfriend. Because of this, the plans she has made for her future have crumbled. She’s a poor girl who cares for her grandmother and had the intention of becoming the village’s first nurse. Rapes were a growing crime in Sierra Leone, and because of this support systems for young women like Heela came to fruition. Kadiatu: Her story revolves around grief and tradition. She lost all 7 of her children. Instead of seeking proper medical attention, her family encouraged her to see a traditional healer. The healer would only speak to her husband and only give superstitious advice for her experience. Her chapter shows how limited healthcare can negatively affect women and children. Tetteh: “My Punishment” is a story about a pregnant woman who was imprisoned under an unequal legal system. She was being severely abused by her late husband, and through an act of self-defense she accidently killed him. She was left in a cell to deal with her pregnancy pains on her own, with limited medical attention. Their system criminalized this woman and locked her away without a trail. While in prison she faces life-threatening conditions and is worried about her baby’s well-being. Her story talks about gender inequality and unfair legal action. All four of these stories were told through a digital comic book. It’s easier for the audience to understand what these women went through, with the visuals that were provided to us. It’s hard for many people who haven’t been through these kinds of experiences to put themselves in these victims' shoes and the illustrations in their stories help close that gap.
