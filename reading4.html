Reading Responses

Reading 4: " Nuclear War: The Rising Risk, and How We Stop It" – W.J Hennigan

In the New York Times article “Nuclear War: The Rising Risk, and How We Stop It,” journalist W.J. Hennigan argues that the world is currently facing the greatest danger of nuclear conflict since the Cold War. The article explains growing conflict between \ Russia, China, and the United States have made the possibility of nuclear war more realistic. The article stresses that governments are actively preparing for the possibility that nuclear weapons could be used in Ukraine. The article focuses heavily on Russia’s invasion of Ukraine. According to the report, U.S. intelligence officials in 2022 believed there was a serious chance Russia might launch a nuclear strike if Ukraine threatened Russian. The United States and Ukraine began preparing emergency responses, including distributing radiation detectors, training medical workers, and stockpiling potassium iodide tablets to reduce radiation damage. These protocols sound like a scene out of the game “Fall out”. The article also explains how modern nuclear weapons are far more destructive and unpredictable than conventional weapons. Even a small nuclear bomb could instantly kill thousands of people, destroy infrastructure, contaminate land with radiation, and overwhelm hospitals and emergency systems. The graphic designer for the article did a great job depicting what this would look like. The illustrations weren’t something i was expecting from such a serious article. I believe seeing what could happen in the near future will make readers more concerned for this safety. The images can be unsettling, but it’s scary that that could be the reality. Although the images can be disturbing to viewers, from an artist standpoint I really enjoyed how interactive the website was. The way the image of the explosion would evolve with each scroll was quite satisfying. And the data graph, with the descriptions, was cool to look at as well. I also enjoyed that the article was a mix of media, with the video as well. Although this is a very concerning topic, I like how graphic designers can intensify emotions through illustrations.
